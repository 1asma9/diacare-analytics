How it works

The data: {{ '{:,}'.format(m.n_total) }} first hospital stays of diabetic patients in 130 US hospitals (1999-2008). The target is a readmission within 30 days ({{(m.base_rate*100)|round(0)|int}} % of the stays). The model is a logistic regression on the 20 fields above ({{m.n_features}} columns once the categories are encoded), trained on {{ '{:,}'.format(m.n_train) }} stays and tested on {{ '{:,}'.format(m.n_test) }} stays it never saw.

The follow-up threshold ({{(thr*100)|round(1)}} %) was chosen on the training data: it is the cut-off that catches the most early readmissions while keeping at least 62 % of all predictions correct. On the test set it flags {{(m.flagged*100)|round(0)|int}} % of the patients and catches {{(m.recall*100)|round(0)|int}} % of the early readmissions.

Limits. The information available at discharge only predicts readmission moderately: the models we tried end between {{'%.2f'|format(comparison[-1].auc_roc)}} and {{'%.2f'|format(comparison[0].auc_roc)}} AUC. Precision is low because only {{(m.base_rate*100)|round(0)|int}} % of patients come back: a model that always answers "no readmission" would be right {{(m.accuracy_always_no*100)|round(0)|int}} % of the time and useless. Data is from 1999-2008 US hospitals and may not match another hospital today. Use the score to rank patients, not to make a decision on one patient.
